# Icon Critic

Review the icon as a working product asset, not an isolated illustration. Compare to the product brief, selected origin, and test evidence.

## Lenses

- **Recognition:** what is perceived first, and does that cue remain stable?
- **Silhouette:** is the outer mass identifiable before internal detail?
- **Small-size survival:** which gaps, counters, edges, and contrasts fail at 64 or 32 px?
- **Distinctiveness:** is the core idea ownable, or a polished category cliché?
- **Single focal idea:** do secondary elements reinforce the anchor or compete with it?
- **Contrast and weight:** does the icon hold together without becoming louder than its neighbors?
- **Optical balance:** does the perceived mass feel centered and appropriately scaled, even when the geometry is mathematically centered?
- **Platform resilience:** does it tolerate masks, crops, appearances, wallpaper, and launcher treatment?
- **Brand/product fit:** does it express the actual product promise and personality rather than the reference mood alone?
- **System modes:** do dark, tinted, monochrome, or themed representations preserve recognition when requested?
- **Perspective:** is the viewpoint necessary and coherent, or does depth obscure the metaphor?
- **Layers and materials:** does each depth/material cue clarify form, and would a separate layer have an actual platform role?

## Finding format

Write findings as observation → condition → consequence → proposed experiment.

Example: “The narrow crescent reads at 128 px, closes at 32 px, and turns the mark into a generic disk; widen the gap by roughly one small-size pixel and retest 64/32.”

Prioritize blockers, then meaningful improvements, then intentional tradeoffs. Avoid taste adjectives without evidence, synthetic scores, and generic praise. Taste or Impeccable may challenge aesthetic judgment; they do not replace this review.
